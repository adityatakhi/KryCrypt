Best practices

Security tips

Habits that actually protect you

🔑

Use a password manager

You only need to remember one strong master password. The manager handles everything else — unique, complex passwords for every site.

🔢

Longer beats complex

A 20-character lowercase phrase is exponentially harder to crack than a 10-character mix of symbols and numbers. Length is your biggest lever.

💬

Try passphrases

Four random words like "apple-brook-cedar-4271" are both memorable and strong — 60+ bits of entropy and easy to type on a phone or keyboard.

🧩

Never reuse passwords

When one site is breached, attackers try your credentials everywhere. Unique passwords mean a breach on one site can't cascade to others.

📲

Enable 2FA everywhere

Two-factor authentication stops most account takeover attacks even when your password is compromised. Use an authenticator app, not SMS.

🔄

Change passwords after breaches

Check haveibeenpwned.com regularly. Change any password for accounts that appear in known data breaches immediately.

🚫

Avoid personal info

Birthdates, pet names, and sports teams are the first things attackers guess — especially when your social media is public.

🧠

Remember a passphrase, not a password

Use a passphrase as your master password — it's the one you must remember. Pick 5+ unrelated words and commit them to memory.
